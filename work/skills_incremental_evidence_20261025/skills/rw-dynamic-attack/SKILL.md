# Dynamic ranking
Use [contract](../shared/contract.md) and [recipes](../shared/recipes.md).
Return a best-first prefix of at most six IDs; the executor can attempt at most six, so a full permutation is unnecessary. Every ranked ID needs one concrete evidence-based reason.
Prefer low response margin supported by interaction timing, background clearance and actual profile evidence. Do not maximize acceleration itself or assert inevitable collision. TTC beyond three seconds is extrapolated, not a sampled encounter; null TTC is not proof of impossibility.
Small terminal offsets such as 0.03 versus 0.26 metres are both small. Do not label one a significant lane departure without a supplied corridor test. The family label is an intent classification, not proof of real merge topology.

## Opportunity-first ranking
Choose a supported candidate when a usable catalog is supplied; never refuse merely because the profile is disabled or a proxy risk is null. Prefer relevant active-window closeness/closing evidence, then compatible later opportunities. A high acceleration, small terminal lateral offset or family label alone is insufficient. Cite exact facts and label later_forecast_only/exploratory_probe honestly. Never promise collision or low reaction margin without execution evidence.

## Evidence-led choice before risk prose
Prefer a usable candidate that adds supported active-window interaction, subject to unchanged background and physical checks. Explain safety-margin and time-horizon tradeoffs explicitly.
If all current candidate proxies weaken active proximity, do not claim that braking or large acceleration automatically adds danger. Later or uncertain exploratory probes remain possible and must be labeled.
Current recipes may include motion-aligned acceleration and active-window sparse intent alignment. They are generated/screened by code and remain prototypes; LLM only ranks IDs.
Keep numeric facts in referenced cards; use a short qualitative reasoning statement and never call the CV reference a real policy counterfactual.
